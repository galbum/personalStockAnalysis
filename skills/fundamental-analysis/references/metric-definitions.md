# Reference: Metric Definitions & Interpretation

Formulas and "what good looks like" for every metric, grouped by pillar. **All interpretation is
sector-relative** — the thresholds below are rough orientation, not universal rules. A PhD analyst reads
each number against the company's industry, history, and peers.

## 1. Profitability

- **Revenue growth (YoY / CAGR)** = (period rev ÷ prior period rev) − 1. *Read:* direction and
  acceleration matter more than the level; separate organic from acquired growth.
- **Gross margin** = (revenue − COGS) ÷ revenue. *Read:* the structural ceiling on profitability; pricing
  power and unit economics. Software/luxury high; distribution/hardware low.
- **Operating margin** = operating income ÷ revenue. *Read:* core efficiency after opex; rising margin on
  rising revenue = operating leverage.
- **Net margin** = net income ÷ revenue. *Read:* bottom-line take, but exposed to one-offs, tax, and
  interest — cross-check against operating margin.
- **EPS (diluted, GAAP)** = net income to common ÷ diluted shares. *Read:* per-share earning power; watch
  the GAAP-vs-adjusted gap and share-count changes (dilution vs buybacks).
- **EBITDA** = operating income + D&A (≈). *Read:* capital-structure- and D&A-neutral profitability; good
  for cross-peer comparison; can flatter capital-heavy firms by ignoring real capex.

## 2. Valuation

- **P/E (trailing)** = price ÷ TTM diluted EPS. *Read:* dollars paid per dollar of past earnings;
  meaningless on near-zero/negative earnings.
- **Forward P/E** = price ÷ next-FY consensus EPS. *Read:* the market's forward expectation; depends on
  estimate quality.
- **P/S** = market cap ÷ revenue. *Read:* useful for unprofitable/high-growth names; must be paired with
  margins (a high P/S is only justified by a path to high-margin profit).
- **EV/EBITDA** = enterprise value ÷ EBITDA, where EV = market cap + total debt − cash. *Read:* the cleanest
  cross-capital-structure multiple; preferred for comparing leveraged peers.
- **PEG** — computed two ways: **(a) primary** = trailing P/E ÷ consensus 3-year EPS CAGR; **(b) cross-check**
  = forward P/E (NTM) ÷ consensus forward EPS growth. *Read:* multiple contextualized by growth; ~1
  sometimes called "fair", but crude and sector-dependent. A forward PEG well below the trailing PEG implies
  the market expects an earnings inflection; above implies deceleration. Interpret, don't worship.
- **P/B** = price ÷ book value per share. *Read:* central for banks/insurers/asset-heavy; weak signal for
  asset-light/IP-driven firms where book understates value.

*Always read multiples three ways: vs peers, vs the company's own history, and vs growth (PEG).*

## 3. Cash Flow

- **Operating cash flow (OCF)** = cash generated by operations (from the cash-flow statement). *Read:*
  compare to net income — persistent OCF < net income flags earnings quality.
- **Free cash flow (FCF)** = OCF − capex. *Read:* cash actually available to investors; the number that
  ultimately backs dividends, buybacks, and debt paydown.
- **FCF margin** = FCF ÷ revenue. *Read:* efficiency of converting sales into free cash; trend matters.
- **FCF yield** = FCF ÷ market cap (state if using EV instead). *Read:* the cash-flow earnings yield;
  compare to risk-free and peer yields — high yield can signal value, thin yield prices in growth.
- **Capex intensity** = capex ÷ revenue. *Read:* asset-light vs capital-heavy; distinguish growth vs
  maintenance capex when disclosed.

## 4. Financial Health

- **Debt-to-equity** = total debt ÷ shareholders' equity. *Read:* leverage; calibrate to sector (banks/
  utilities high by design). Buybacks shrink equity and can inflate D/E artificially.
- **Interest coverage** = EBIT ÷ interest expense. *Read:* how comfortably operating profit services debt;
  <3x at a cyclical business is a danger zone. Pair with the EBITDA −30% recessionary stress test.
- **Quick ratio** = (current assets − inventory) ÷ current liabilities. *Read:* stricter liquidity test
  than the current ratio; a wide current-vs-quick gap points to inventory-heavy liquidity.
- **Net cash / net debt** = cash & equivalents − total debt. *Read:* net cash = strategic optionality and
  resilience; net debt = obligation that must be serviced from cash flow.
- **Net debt / EBITDA** = net debt ÷ EBITDA. *Read:* leverage in cash-flow terms; >3–4x is elevated for
  most non-financials, but sector-dependent.
- **Current ratio** = current assets ÷ current liabilities. *Read:* short-term liquidity; <1 is a flag
  unless the business is structurally cash-generative (e.g. some retailers).
- **ROE** = net income ÷ shareholders' equity. *Read:* return to owners; **decompose via DuPont**
  (net margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier) — high ROE from leverage alone is lower quality.
- **ROIC** = NOPAT ÷ invested capital (debt + equity − cash, roughly). *Read:* the headline capital-
  efficiency metric. Compare to WACC: **ROIC > WACC creates value; ROIC < WACC destroys it.** Durable
  high ROIC is a moat signal.

## 5. Forward Signals

- **Management guidance** — company's own forward outlook. *Read:* track record of meeting/beating;
  raised vs cut vs reaffirmed; tone.
- **Analyst consensus** — rating distribution, price target (implied up/downside), consensus estimates.
  *Read:* sentiment gauge; targets are noisy — use the distribution, not a single number.
- **Earnings revisions** — net direction and breadth of estimate changes (last 30–90d). *Read:* rising,
  broad-based upward revisions are among the more robust forward signals; the reverse warns.
- **Share buybacks** — authorization, execution pace, net share-count trend. *Read:* real reduction
  (accretive) vs merely offsetting stock-comp dilution; check funding source (FCF vs debt).
- **Insider transactions** — open-market buys/sells by officers/directors. *Read:* cluster buying is a
  meaningful positive; scheduled (10b5-1) selling is largely noise — separate them.
- **Short interest** — shares sold short as a % of float, plus days-to-cover (shares short ÷ avg daily
  volume). *Read:* >10% of float is elevated, >20% is short-squeeze territory on a positive catalyst;
  rising short interest *without* a falling price is informed money pressing a bearish bet. Read the
  12-month trend, not just the level.
